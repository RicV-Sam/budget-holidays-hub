Editorial Standards

Accuracy, first-hand transparency and useful local context

Budget Holidays Hub exists to help visitors make better-value travel decisions. The new UK guides are built around clear evidence, not claims of authority.

1. Experience must be labelled honestly

Living in the UK is not the same as personally testing every route. Pages distinguish local research, a personal visit and a deliberately field-tested guide. We do not use first-hand language for desk research.

2. Changing facts need a source and date

Entry rules, fares, timetables, closures and official safety advice link to the organisation responsible for them. Point-in-time prices are dated and described as checks, not promises.

3. Recommendations explain the trade-off

A recommendation should say who it suits, what it costs in time or money and when another option is better. “Hidden gem,” “must-see” and “best” are not substitutes for evidence.

4. Field notes record the inconvenient details

  • Door-to-door journey time rather than the advertised segment.
  • Luggage, steps, seating, toilets and accessibility barriers.
  • Queues, crowding and a workable poor-weather alternative.
  • The visit date, actual spend and any complimentary element.

5. Commercial relationships are visible

Editorial advice is not changed to improve affiliate revenue. A complimentary stay, ticket, press trip or commercial relationship must be disclosed close to the relevant recommendation. See the Affiliate Disclosure.

6. Corrections

Travel information changes. If you spot an error, send the page and the detail that needs checking. Material corrections are made promptly and the review date changes only when the page has been substantively rechecked.

7. Legacy guides are not given artificial freshness

Older holidays-from-the-UK pages may remain useful for planning, but their historical price ranges are not live quotes. Until a guide receives a substantive evidence review, it carries a visible planning-estimate notice. Unrelated archive sections remain available to existing visitors but are excluded from search indexing so the site can maintain a clear travel focus.
