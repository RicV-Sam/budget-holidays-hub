The local advantage

Advice that can be checked on the ground

Being based in the UK makes it possible to test the journey, not just summarise it: the station transfer, the walk from a “central” hotel, the queues, the wet-weather backup and whether a day trip still feels worth it after the return journey.

That creates a more useful kind of guide—one that tells visitors what worked, what did not and who should choose a different route.

What a field note records
  • Exact route and journey time
  • What was paid and when
  • Accessibility and luggage friction
  • What was crowded, closed or overrated
  • What is worth repeating

Evidence labels

Readers should know how each guide was made

Local knowledge is valuable, but it should not be exaggerated. Each UK guide uses one of three plain labels.

Read the full method →
  • Locally researchedWritten from the UK using current official information, route checks and local context.
  • Personally visitedThe writer has been there; the date and limits of that experience are stated.
  • Field testedThe published route, costs and practical details were deliberately checked for the guide.
  • Official check requiredEntry rules, fares, closures and timetables link to the responsible authority.
