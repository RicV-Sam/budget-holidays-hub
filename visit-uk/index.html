Visit the UK on a budget

A practical first-trip guide to Britain: what to check before you fly, how to choose a sensible route and where local knowledge can save money without making the trip harder.

Reviewed 11 August 2026 · Rules and fares linked to official sources

The short answer

For a first budget trip, give London enough time to justify the arrival cost, then add no more than one well-connected region. Book the long intercity journey early if the plan is fixed; use coaches when price matters more than time; and avoid choosing an airport or hotel from the headline price alone.

Before you travel: check the rule, not a screenshot

Most visitors need either an electronic travel authorisation (ETA) or a visa, depending on nationality and circumstances. Because this is a legal requirement that can change, use the official checker before buying a non-refundable journey. Do not pay a lookalike application website when the government service is available directly.

Choose a route that survives the real journey times

The UK looks compact on a map, but a hotel checkout, station transfer and luggage can consume half a day. A useful first itinerary is usually a base plus one extension—not London, Edinburgh, the Cotswolds, Liverpool and Bath compressed into a week.

3–4 nights: one base

Stay in London or another arrival city. Use one easy day trip only if it supports your main reason for visiting.

5–7 nights: city plus one

Combine London with a well-connected second stop such as York, Bath, Brighton or Edinburgh. Check the full transfer before deciding.

8–12 nights: two regions

Add a slower regional stay. This is where a car can help—but only outside places where parking and traffic become the trip.

Train, coach or car: pick for the journey you have

There is no single cheapest method across Britain. The right choice depends on how fixed your dates are, how much luggage you carry, how far the station is from the actual destination and what your holiday time is worth.

ModeBest whenMain catch

Local planning rule: compare hotel-to-hotel time, not station-to-station time.

Beyond London: add contrast, not a checklist

The best second stop should feel different from London and fit the direction you are already travelling. These are planning archetypes, not a claim that one destination suits everyone.

  • Fast contrast: Brighton for coast and an easy rail journey.
  • Historic compact city: Bath or York for a walkable centre and a clear overnight focus.
  • Scotland introduction: Edinburgh when the longer rail journey or an open-jaw flight works with the itinerary.
  • Rural extension: choose a specific part of the Cotswolds, Lake District, Wales or Scotland only after solving local transport.
