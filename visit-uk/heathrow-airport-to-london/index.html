Heathrow to London: Choose the Right Train for Your Hotel

Compare the Piccadilly line, Elizabeth line and Heathrow Express by London destination, luggage needs and total journey cost.

Published and researched 5 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based · England, Wales and Scotland

The short answer

Start with the station nearest your hotel. The Piccadilly line is Heathrow’s budget rail option; the Elizabeth line is useful for several central destinations; Heathrow Express is fastest to Paddington. The fastest airport train is not necessarily the fastest or cheapest journey to your room.

Choose by hotel location

Paddington: compare the Elizabeth line with Heathrow Express using the actual ticket offer. A cheaper Express advance offer can change the decision; do not assume the walk-up price applies to every booking.

Farringdon or Liverpool Street: a through Elizabeth line journey can avoid a change at Paddington. Confirm the train’s destination and the final walk.

South Kensington or near a Piccadilly line stop: compare the direct Tube journey with a faster train that requires a second service. With luggage, one longer ride may be easier.

Elsewhere: enter the accommodation address in the planner, not simply “central London”. Check exits, stairs and lift availability where these affect your trip. Our London guide explains the accommodation trade-offs.

Compare actual fares before tapping in

Use the TfL single fare finder for your terminal station, London destination and route. Check Heathrow Express separately through its official booking link. Airport fares and premium services should not be assumed to fit a central London daily cap.

Price the whole party and the onward trip. If an Express quote saves ten minutes on the first train but introduces a change, it may not save time at the hotel. Record the travel date, ticket restrictions and whether the quote includes the onward leg before comparing it.

Eligible journeys accept contactless or Oyster, but these payment methods are not interchangeable with every ticket product. Use the same card or device for one journey. See contactless versus Oyster for overseas visitors for foreign-card and family considerations.

From arrivals to the right platform

  1. Confirm your terminal and follow signs for your chosen service. Heathrow has separate rail and Underground options; a train sign alone is not enough.
  2. Check the service destination, terminal arrangements and live departure display before boarding.
  3. Keep the payment card or ticket you used available through the journey.
  4. At your London stop, check the right exit and your hotel walk before leaving the station.

If you need step-free access, check the complete route and current lift status. A step-free airport station does not establish that the destination platform, street exit or hotel is accessible.

Late flights and disruption

Do not rely on a single published “last train” time. Services depend on date, terminal, destination and engineering work. Search after allowing for border formalities and baggage. If rail is unavailable, compare an official coach option, a licensed taxi or an airport-area overnight stay using the live price.

Keep the hotel informed about a late arrival. When the flight home is early, an easier final-night location may be worth more than squeezing in another city. Add transfers to your whole-trip budget before choosing the flight.

Sources and checks
