UK Supermarkets for Tourists: Cheap Groceries, Meal Deals and Ready Meals

How to use British supermarkets for affordable breakfasts, picnic lunches and selected dinners—without missing the pubs, restaurants and local food worth travelling for.

Published and checked 28 August 2026 · Branch ranges and prices vary

The short answer

Aldi and Lidl are normally the cheapest choices for a full grocery shop. Tesco is the best all-round option for most travellers because it combines broad coverage, meal deals, ready meals and everyday essentials. M&S Food and Waitrose cost more but are our leading choices for a better prepared dinner. Co-op, Tesco Express and Sainsbury’s Local are useful for late arrivals and top-ups, although a full-sized supermarket will usually give you better value.

British people usually say supermarket, food shop or convenience store; international visitors may know the same shops as grocery stores. The cheapest shop is not automatically the best value for a traveller: location, usable portion sizes, cooking facilities and wasted food all matter.

Which UK supermarket is cheapest?

Which? named Aldi the cheapest supermarket in its July 2026 comparison, with Lidl close behind. Its comparison tracks a defined list rather than every possible holiday shop, and the ranking can change monthly.

Aldi: cheapest overall for the main shop

Aldi is a strong first stop for cereal, yoghurt, fruit, bread, drinks, snacks and simple meals. Its own-brand range keeps a larger shop economical, but the selection is narrower than Tesco or Sainsbury’s and central-city branches can be harder to find. It works best when you can reach a full store and buy several days’ supplies at once.

Lidl: best value for bakery food and picnics

Lidl is usually close to Aldi on price and is often the easier choice where Aldi is absent. The in-store bakery makes it particularly useful for rolls, pastries and picnic food, while fruit, cold meats, cheese, snacks and drinks suit a road trip. The useful distinction is value: a bakery lunch you will actually eat can beat a marginally cheaper basket elsewhere.

Best all-round supermarkets for visitors

Tesco: the most practical overall choice

Tesco combines full-sized supermarkets, smaller Express branches, meal deals, chilled dinners, budget and premium own brands, vegetarian, vegan and free-from products, toiletries and travel supplies. A large Tesco is suitable for a full shop; Tesco Express is a smaller, more convenient format and can cost more for equivalent items. Current Clubcard prices can differ from the standard shelf price.

Sainsbury’s: a strong alternative

Sainsbury’s fills a similar role, with a broad prepared-food range and useful own-brand choices. Smaller Sainsbury’s Local shops are helpful in cities but should not be treated as the same value proposition as a supermarket. Nectar offers can make selected products cheaper, but the non-loyalty price is the safer budget assumption for a visitor.

Asda and Morrisons: useful for families

Asda and Morrisons suit a bigger self-catering shop or a family buying several breakfasts and dinners. They are often outside city centres or near retail parks, so compare the saving with the journey. Some Morrisons branches have bakeries, cafés or fresh counters, but facilities vary and should be checked for the actual store.

Best UK supermarkets for ready meals

M&S Food: our leading ready-meal recommendation

M&S Food is not normally the place to buy the cheapest milk or cereal. Its value is different: chilled curries, fresh pasta, gastropub-style meals, pizza, salads, desserts, cheese and picnic food can replace a restaurant or takeaway dinner. Dinner-for-two combinations can work especially well when two travellers can share every part of the offer. Check the current store promotion before relying on a Dine In price.

Waitrose: premium food for a cottage or apartment

Waitrose is our other quality recommendation for prepared meals, bakery food, cheese, wine and better ingredients. It is appealing in a self-catering cottage where dinner at home is part of the evening, but it is usually expensive for the whole general shop. Its official meal-deal page shows that combinations and prices vary by offer.

Convenience stores are useful—but usually more expensive

Co-op, Tesco Express, Sainsbury’s Local, Morrisons Daily, SPAR, Nisa, Londis, One Stop and petrol-station shops are excellent for bread, milk, breakfast supplies, sandwiches, meal deals, drinks, toiletries and forgotten essentials. They solve late arrivals and one-night stays. Use a larger supermarket for the main shop whenever the time and journey make sense.

Traveller tip: “Express”, “Local” and convenience-store branches are usually smaller and can charge more for equivalent products than a full-sized supermarket. Use them for top-ups rather than a complete holiday shop.

What to buy for your accommodation

Hotel room with no kitchen

Choose food that is ready to eat: sandwiches, wraps, salads, sushi, cooked chicken, cold meats, cheese, hummus, rolls, bakery food, fruit, yoghurt, grain salads, pork pies, sausage rolls, Scotch eggs, desserts and meal deals.

Check for a fridge first. Buy chilled food for immediate use if the room cannot keep it cold; never store it unrefrigerated.

Accommodation with a microwave

Look for curries with rice, lasagne, pasta, cottage pie, fish pie, soup, chilli, macaroni cheese, microwave rice and prepared vegetables.

Read the cooking instructions before paying. Some chilled meals look microwaveable but require an oven.

Apartment, cottage or full kitchen

Fresh pizza, prepared curry, oven-ready chicken, fresh pasta and sauce, vegetables, salad, garlic bread, frozen meals and rotisserie chicken where available can make an easy dinner. Add breakfast and next-day picnic supplies.

You do not need to cook from scratch to get value from self-catering accommodation.

Meal deals, dinner offers and yellow stickers

Lunch meal deals

A UK supermarket meal deal normally combines one main, one snack and one drink from marked products. Tesco’s official meal-deal range showed different standard and Clubcard prices when checked. Exact combinations, premium tiers and loyalty discounts vary, so follow the shelf labels rather than assuming any three products qualify.

Dinner offers

Depending on the shop, a dinner offer might combine a main and side, pizza and sides, pasta and sauce, or a dinner for two with dessert. Treat the current promotion as a bonus rather than the foundation of the budget.

Reduced or “yellow-sticker” food

Food approaching its use-by date may be reduced, sometimes more heavily later in the day. Availability and timing are never guaranteed. It can be useful for a same-day meal, but respect the use-by date and keep chilled products cold.

Supermarket shopping on a UK road trip

Stop at a full-sized supermarket on the edge of a town or near a retail park before entering a remote area. Buy water, snacks and picnic food before motorway journeys, carry a reusable bag, and use a small cool bag for chilled products. Never leave chilled food in a warm vehicle.

Check parking signs, then fill the food bag and fuel tank before national parks, remote coastlines or islands. Our UK motorway fuel guide explains how to plan a safe, lower-cost stop without making a long detour.

Shopping in rural areas

Supermarkets and convenience stores are normally easy to find in UK cities, towns and established tourist areas. In remote parts of Scotland, Wales, Cornwall, national parks and the islands, shops can be much farther apart and may close earlier. Village shops are extremely useful but can have a smaller selection, higher prices and limited Sunday hours. Buy supplies before travelling into an isolated area and check the specific branch.

Sunday and holiday opening hours

  • England and Wales: large shops can generally trade for six consecutive hours between 10:00 and 18:00 on Sundays. Small shops are not subject to the same restriction.
  • Scotland: the same Sunday trading-hour restriction does not apply.
  • Northern Ireland: separate rules apply; large shops normally trade for a shorter Sunday window.
  • Everywhere: branches can choose shorter hours, and Christmas, Easter and bank-holiday hours can differ.

Check the exact branch rather than relying on a chain’s general hours. The rules are explained by GOV.UK and the Northern Ireland Department for Communities.

Loyalty prices: useful, but not guaranteed for visitors

Tesco Clubcard, Sainsbury’s Nectar, Lidl Plus, Co-op Membership, Morrisons More, myWaitrose and M&S Sparks can change the price of selected products. Always compare the standard and loyalty prices before building the day around an offer.

Do not assume every international visitor can join. Tesco’s current Clubcard terms require members to have a valid UK address. Lidl Plus requires an account and mobile-number verification; other programmes can require an app, minimum age, account details or local eligibility. The simplest visitor budget uses standard prices and treats any accessible discount as a saving.

Practical supermarket tips for a UK trip

  • Check the unit price and compare loyalty with non-loyalty prices.
  • Bring a reusable bag; expect to pay if you need a disposable one.
  • Check use-by dates, cooking instructions and whether your room has a fridge.
  • Buy smaller portions if leftovers cannot be stored safely.
  • Use supermarkets for everyday breakfasts, lunches and selected dinners, then keep money for a pub meal or local speciality that matters to you.

Our practical verdict

For the cheapest general shop, start with Aldi or Lidl. Tesco is the easiest all-round recommendation for most visitors. For a better evening meal without restaurant prices, M&S Food and Waitrose are our preferred prepared-food options. Co-op and smaller Express or Local stores are ideal when convenience matters more than the lowest possible price.

UK supermarket questions

What is the cheapest supermarket in the UK?

Aldi or Lidl is normally the first place to look. Which? ranked Aldi cheapest in July 2026, but monthly baskets and individual products vary.

Which UK supermarket has the best ready meals?

M&S Food and Waitrose are our premium recommendations. Tesco and Sainsbury’s provide wider price ranges and stronger general-shop value.

What is the best supermarket for tourists?

Tesco is the most practical all-round choice because of its range and store formats. The best answer still depends on your location, kitchen and length of stay.

Are Tesco Express and Sainsbury’s Local more expensive?

Convenience branches commonly cost more for equivalent items and carry a smaller range. Use them for top-ups rather than assuming they match a full supermarket.

What can I eat without a kitchen?

Sandwiches, salads, sushi, cooked meats, cheese, bakery food, fruit, yoghurt and picnic supplies all work. Only buy chilled food when you can keep it cold or eat it immediately.

What is a supermarket meal deal?

It normally means one marked main, one snack and one drink for a combined price. Qualifying products, prices and loyalty discounts vary.

Are supermarkets open on Sundays?

Yes, but the rules differ across the four UK nations and individual shops can shorten their hours. Check the exact branch before travelling.

Is M&S cheaper than a restaurant?

M&S is expensive compared with Aldi or Lidl, but a prepared dinner can still cost less than a restaurant or takeaway. Compare the full meal, not the price of one component.
