Land's End on a budget

The commercial visitor complex can feel like a tourist trap. The headland is another story: wild Atlantic water, huge granite cliffs and one of the best short coast walks in Cornwall.

Visited and facts checked 24 August 2026 · Original photography throughout

The short answer

Land's End is worth visiting for the landscape, not because you need a ticketed attraction. Entry to the headland and coast paths is free, but the official car park currently costs £9.50 for the day. Budget visitors can start in Sennen, pay less at a marked car park and walk the coast path in—or arrive by bus from Penzance. Bring proper shoes, a layer for the wind and enough time to continue south beyond the shops.

Is Land's End worth it?

Yes—if you go for the coast. The name draws crowds, and the arrival area leans hard into that popularity with souvenir shops, food outlets, family attractions and a paid signpost photo. If you expect a lonely edge of Britain, that first impression may disappoint.

None of that obliges you to spend more. Visit Cornwall confirms that there is no admission charge to walk into Land's End, and the coast paths are accessible throughout the year. Pass through the commercial area, find the South West Coast Path and the mood changes within minutes.

Our honest take: the visitor complex is optional infrastructure. Toilets and refreshments are useful; the dramatic coastline is the attraction. Set your budget before arriving and it is easy to separate the two.

How to avoid paying £9.50 to park

The official Land's End car park lists a full-day tariff of £9.50, with £5 evening parking after 4pm and the first 20 minutes free. Prices vary seasonally, so check the official page and the signs when you arrive.

For a budget day, our preferred idea is to stop before the attraction and make the approach part of the walk. Sennen is the practical starting point: the recognised Sennen Cove and Land's End route is 3.3 miles (5.3 km), graded moderate, and begins at Sennen Cove car park.

Three ways to make the walk the main event

Sennen out and back

3.3 miles / 5.3 km, moderate. Start by the harbour or Mayon car park, climb to the headland and follow the Coast Path to Land's End. Return by the coast or use the more level inland trail where appropriate.

South to the rock arch

From Land's End, follow the Coast Path south towards Pordenack Point. An out-and-back lets you turn around when time or weather says so, while still reaching the quieter cliffs and views of Enys Dodnan.

Land's End and Nanjizal

3 miles / 4.9 km, described as easy. The published circular continues south to Nanjizal before returning inland through farmland. Some inland paths are not signed throughout, so carry a proper route.

We walked south from the main headland. It is the simplest way to turn a quick landmark stop into a memorable half-day: the crowds thin, the view opens and each bend reveals another cove, sea stack or fractured granite wall.

What to look for beyond the signpost

The Longships Lighthouse

The lighthouse stands on the Longships Rocks just over a mile offshore. It is easy to pick out from the main headland and repeatedly reappears as you walk south. The rocks and broken water around it make clear why this corner of Cornwall has such a formidable maritime reputation.

The Isles of Scilly—if the air is clear

The Isles of Scilly lie around 28 miles off the Cornish coast. On a genuinely clear day they appear as a thin, low line rather than a dramatic island skyline. We could see them, but only faintly; binoculars or a zoom lens help, and haze can erase them completely.

Enys Dodnan and the granite coast

South of the main viewpoint, the rock arch at Enys Dodnan is the visual reward for continuing along the path. Keep to the marked route and view it from safe ground—the scale is best understood with the sea moving through the opening below.

Getting to Land's End as a UK visitor

For an international trip, treat Land's End as part of a Cornwall stay, not a day trip from London. Penzance is the useful rail and bus hub. Stay locally for at least a night, then use public transport or a car for the final stretch.

  • By train and bus: travel to Penzance, then check Transport for Cornwall services 1 or 1A to Land's End. Routes and times can change, so plan both the outward and return journey on the day.
  • By car: the A30 ends at Land's End. The final roads can feel narrow to drivers unfamiliar with Cornwall, and UK traffic drives on the left.
  • For a one-way walk: buses also connect Land's End with Sennen and, on selected services, St Just or St Ives. A bus-and-walk combination avoids retracing the whole route.

Good use of a Cornwall day: arrive early, walk from Sennen to Land's End, continue south for as long as conditions allow, then return for the bus or your parked car. Pack lunch and water so the visitor-centre prices remain a choice.

Accessibility and facilities

The visitor complex has parking, toilets, food and a formal access statement, making it the most practical choice for visitors who need to minimise walking. The wild Coast Path is a different environment: expect uneven ground, exposed sections and slopes.

The South West Coast Path guide notes that an alternative section of the First and Last Trail near Sennen is well surfaced and suitable for many wheelchairs and pushchairs, but it is not a promise that the entire coastal route is step-free. Check the attraction's current access statement and choose the route around the person with the most limited mobility.

What to take and how to walk safely

  • Wear walking shoes, not flip-flops. Even short sections can be rocky, muddy or slippery.
  • Carry a windproof or waterproof layer. Conditions on an Atlantic headland can change quickly, even when Penzance feels calm.
  • Stay back from cliff edges. Some cliffs overhang or are unstable; a photograph is never worth stepping beyond a barrier or worn path.
  • Bring water, food and sun protection. Sea breezes can disguise strong sun, and facilities become sparse once you leave the complex.
  • Keep children and dogs close. The path crosses open cliff and, on longer loops, working farmland.
  • Download the route. Mobile reception can be patchy. For a coastal emergency, call 999 or 112 and ask for the Coastguard.

A simple budget half-day

  1. Park legally in Sennen or take the bus. Check the return time before setting off.
  2. Walk the coast path to Land's End. Pause above the harbour and look back across Whitesand Bay.
  3. Use the facilities, skip the extras you do not value. There is no admission charge just to enter the headland.
  4. Continue south. Look back for the Longships, then carry on towards Pordenack Point and Enys Dodnan.
  5. Turn around with energy in reserve. Wind and uneven ground can make the return slower than the distance suggests.

If you only photograph the famous sign and leave, Land's End may feel overpriced. If you spend the money on a legal parking space or bus fare and give the coast two or three unhurried hours, it becomes one of Cornwall's strongest low-cost days out.
