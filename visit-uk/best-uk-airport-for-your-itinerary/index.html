UK trip planning

Which UK airport should you fly into?

Start with where you want to stay. Manchester, Birmingham or a Scottish airport may save a long transfer when London is not the focus of your holiday.

Published and transport links checked 15 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based

The short answer

Shortlist airports near your first and last overnight stops, then compare the flights available from your departure city on your dates. London fits a London stay; Manchester is worth comparing for north-west England; Birmingham for the West Midlands; and Edinburgh or Glasgow for a trip centred on those Scottish cities. For the Highlands or north-east Scotland, check Inverness or Aberdeen too.

A cheaper flight can lose its saving once you add a long transfer, a late-night taxi or an extra hotel. For a trip that moves across Britain, compare arriving at one airport and flying home from another.

London, Manchester or Birmingham?

London: useful when the capital is part of the plan

If most nights are in London, compare the airport-to-hotel routes before looking further away for a cheaper flight. Our Heathrow guide covers trains, taxis and buses; our Gatwick guide explains terminal transfers and onward options. A London arrival can also fit the start of a journey north, provided you actually want time in London.

Manchester: compare for a northern England stay

Manchester is a logical starting point for a Manchester-based holiday or a north-west England itinerary. The airport has its own railway station, linked to the terminals by walkways. The airport quotes around 20 minutes by train to Manchester Piccadilly; that is train time, not landing-to-hotel time. See Manchester Airport's rail guidance.

For Liverpool, Yorkshire or the Lake District, search the full journey to your accommodation. A route from the airport may be useful, but a rural final leg can change the decision. Compare the actual connections rather than assuming every northern destination is a quick trip from Manchester.

Birmingham: useful for the West Midlands

Birmingham Airport connects to Birmingham International railway station by the free Air-Rail Link. From there, trains serve Birmingham New Street and other destinations. International is the airport station; New Street is a separate city-centre station. Check the airport's rail guidance for both legs. The Air-Rail Link does not run throughout the night.

For a Birmingham or West Midlands stay, compare this route before accepting a London arrival and an additional intercity journey. For a countryside trip, add the local bus, taxi or car-hire cost beyond the railway station.

Which Scottish airport fits your stay?

Edinburgh: city stays and onward rail connections

Edinburgh Airport has a tram into the city. For onward trains, the airport identifies interchanges including Edinburgh Gateway, Edinburgh Park and Haymarket; Waverley is a walk from the St Andrew Square tram stop. Check the tram service and rail connections. A tram stop at the airport is not the same as a mainline station inside the terminal.

For an Edinburgh-focused break, this is a sensible airport to price first. If the holiday is mainly in the Highlands, compare the onward journey with flying to Inverness rather than treating Edinburgh as the destination for all of Scotland.

Glasgow: city stays and a western starting point

Glasgow Airport's Express 500 bus links the terminal with the city. There is no railway station at the terminal: the airport identifies Paisley Gilmour Street as the nearest station, reached using a connecting bus. Check its train-transfer guidance and current bus stops, particularly during roadworks.

Compare Glasgow for a Glasgow-based visit or a journey beginning in western Scotland. If you are heading onwards to a loch, island or rural accommodation, check that connection separately; the airport-to-city service does not solve the whole trip.

Inverness: compare for a Highland itinerary

Inverness can make sense when the holiday starts around Inverness or in the Highlands. The airport lists direct buses to the city. Inverness Airport railway station is separate from the terminal: the airport describes an approximately 20-minute uncovered walk. Compare the bus with the station transfer if you have luggage or limited mobility.

Check the onward route to your exact Highland base before booking. A geographically closer airport is not automatically easier if the flight requires a long connection or the final journey has limited transport.

Aberdeen: for north-east Scotland

For Aberdeen and a north-east Scotland stay, compare an Aberdeen arrival before routing through central Scotland. The airport lists the Jet Bus 727 into the city. Its nearest railway station is Dyce, about two miles from the terminal, requiring a separate transfer. Use Aberdeen Airport's transport guide to compare the city bus with the route via the station.

Let your flight times shape the shortlist

  • A short city break: prioritise an arrival that leaves useful time in your chosen city. A lower flight price is less attractive if the transfer consumes much of the first day.
  • A late landing: search onward transport after allowing for border checks, baggage and the terminal walk. Price a taxi or airport hotel if the usable services have finished.
  • An early flight home: work backwards from your airline's check-in requirements. Include the cost of reaching the airport before normal daytime transport starts.
  • A weekend or public holiday: check the timetable and engineering work for that exact date, not a weekday example.
  • A connection rather than a direct flight: include the layover in the comparison. Check whether all flights are on one booking and the airline's conditions if a delay affects the connection.

Use National Rail for rail journeys and the airport's linked operators for bus and tram times. Fixed last-service times and flight-route lists can become stale, so check live results before paying.

Fly into one airport and home from another

A multi-city booking, sometimes called an open-jaw itinerary, can suit a trip that moves in one direction. For example, compare flying into London, travelling north during the holiday and flying home from Edinburgh with returning to London solely for the flight. The reverse can work too. This is a planning example, not a claim that those flights are available from your home airport.

Compare the complete multi-city quote with a return flight, then add the train, coach, transfer or extra night needed to get back to the original airport. If hiring a car, include any charge for returning it to a different location. The simpler route is worth considering even when the flight itself costs more.

Our seven-day London and York itinerary assumes a return through London. Use it as one option, then compare a route ending elsewhere if that better fits your flights and priorities.

Compare the total, then check the time

Compare: flights for the whole party + baggage and other chosen extras + transport to the first hotel + transport from the last hotel + any extra overnight stay or car-return charge caused by the airport choice.

Keep sightseeing and accommodation plans comparable, and include any intercity journey that changes between options. Record the arrival time at the first hotel as well as the price. A small flight saving may not be worth a lost afternoon; a much larger saving may justify a longer transfer when the schedule is relaxed.

Build the result into your UK trip budget before booking accommodation. Airport choice, route and overnight stops work best when planned together.
