Practical UK travel

Your first 24 hours in the UK

Get connected, reach your accommodation and settle in without making a tired arrival harder than it needs to be.

Published and sources checked 15 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based

The short answer

Before flying, save your hotel address and arrival instructions, arrange mobile data, check your charger, and plan both your transfer and a backup. Leave the first day flexible: landing time is not the time you will reach a station or your room.

What should you save before the flight?

Keep the exact accommodation name, street address, postcode, booking reference and contact number somewhere you can open without a signal. Similar hotel names and airport-branded branches are easy to confuse. Save the agreed check-in date and any late-arrival instructions.

  • Download tickets using the operator's supported offline option; do not assume a screenshot is a valid substitute for every ticket.
  • Save the terminal, onward destination and the final route from the station or stop to the entrance.
  • Keep a separate copy of essential contact details if your phone runs out of power.
  • Check your entry requirements using the ETA and visitor-visa guide.

Roaming, local SIM or travel eSIM?

Compare your home provider's UK roaming allowance with a local SIM or travel eSIM for your actual stay. Check data allowance, validity, activation timing, calls and texts, hotspot use and top-ups. A data-only plan may suit maps and messaging apps but does not necessarily provide an ordinary UK phone number.

Check your exact phone model and country of purchase for eSIM compatibility, and whether it is network-locked. Apple's guidance confirms that an iPhone must be unlocked to use another provider and that the home line can still incur roaming fees when both lines are active. Follow your own manufacturer's instructions for other phones. Apple: using an eSIM abroad.

Install and configure the plan while you have a reliable connection, following its activation rules. Before leaving the airport, test mobile data with Wi-Fi switched off. Check which SIM supplies data and what remains enabled on your home line. Do not assume turning off data roaming also disables all chargeable calls.

Will downloaded maps get you to the hotel?

Not by themselves for every journey. Google Maps' offline mode does not provide public-transport, walking or cycling directions. Offline driving also lacks live traffic and alternative routes. Downloading a map is useful, but it is not a complete offline arrival plan. Google Maps: offline maps and limitations.

Save the transport route separately: line or operator, destination shown on the service, interchange, exit and hotel entrance. A saved walking map can help you orient yourself; check it before leaving a connected area. Recheck live disruption when you have a connection.

What should you bring for charging?

Bring a UK-compatible plug adaptor and the cables your devices use. Check the charger's input label: the UK supply is nominally 230V at 50Hz. A travel adaptor changes the plug connection; it does not convert voltage or frequency. Check the adaptor's power rating and earth compatibility before use. Electrical Safety First: travel adaptors.

Keep your phone charged before departure and carry essential charging equipment where you can reach it. If packing a power bank, check the airline's current battery rules before flying. Do not rely on finding an available socket during the transfer.

What if your flight lands late?

Work backwards from when you can actually leave the terminal, allowing for passport control, baggage and any assistance. Check the last useful connection to your accommodation for the date you travel, not only the last train towards a city centre. A late connection may leave you far from the hotel.

Compare a later coach, a quoted taxi or a first night near the airport. Confirm pickup arrangements and any hotel shuttle hours before paying. Use the Heathrow transfer guide or Gatwick transfer guide for the options; choose the airport around the itinerary if you have not booked yet.

A flight delay should trigger two checks: whether your transport booking remains usable and whether the accommodation can still admit you. Save a reachable contact rather than relying on an unattended message.

Can you go straight to your room?

Ask the property. Morning arrival does not guarantee early room access; baggage storage, early check-in and day rooms each have their own availability and terms. As one supplier example, Premier Inn publishes standard check-in from 3pm and asks guests expecting to arrive after 11pm to notify the hotel. These are its rules, not a UK-wide standard. Premier Inn: booking terms.

If you will arrive just after midnight, give the property the exact date and time and ask which night to reserve. Booking that calendar day's normal afternoon check-in may not cover the hours immediately after midnight. Obtain written confirmation of access and any charges.

For a self-check-in apartment, confirm when the code arrives, whether it works offline, where the entrance is and who answers if it fails. Compare these practical details in choosing UK accommodation by total cost.

Keep the first day easy to change

Make settling in, food and a manageable local outing the priorities. Avoid a tightly timed attraction immediately after landing. Check nearby food opening hours and the room's facilities before buying groceries; the supermarket guide separates no-kitchen and self-catering options.

If travelling with children, luggage or access requirements, use the family and accessible transfer checklist. If collecting a car, allow time to learn it and do not start a drive while tired; see car hire and driving.
