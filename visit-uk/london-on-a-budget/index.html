London on a budget

How to spend less in London without sleeping miles from the places you came to see or losing half the trip to awkward transfers.

Published 28 July 2026 · reviewed 5 September 2026

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based · England, Wales and Scotland

The short answer

Stay near a useful Tube or rail line rather than paying only for a famous postcode. Use the same contactless card or device throughout each journey day, group free sights by area and price the airport-to-hotel transfer before choosing the flight. London can be good value when you stop crossing it unnecessarily.

Flying into Gatwick? Use the local Gatwick terminal and train guide to find the South Terminal station and choose the most useful direct London service.

What to budget for a London day

As an illustrative target, two adults sharing a £120 room would each allocate £60 for accommodation, £25 for food, £9 for local transport and £10 for paid sights: £104 per person for a day with an overnight stay. These are planning assumptions, not live quotes or a measured city average. Flights, airport transfers, entry permission and insurance are extra.

For a solo traveller paying for that same room alone, the example rises to £164. If your room quote is higher, substitute it before booking. The seven-day UK budget shows how to separate nights, days and one-off costs.

Use contactless transport consistently

London’s fare system applies daily caps when you use the same contactless card, phone or Oyster card correctly. Do not alternate between a physical card and the same card in a phone wallet: the system can treat them as different payment methods.

As checked on 5 September 2026, TfL guidance lists a £1.75 bus or tram fare with unlimited bus and tram transfers within one hour, a £5.25 daily bus-and-tram cap, and an £8.90 daily cap for Tube travel in Zones 1–2. Check Transport for London immediately before travel because fares and conditions can change.

A cheap room is poor value if every day begins with two changes or a long walk to the station. Look for a place within a comfortable, well-lit walk of a station with a direct route to the areas you will actually visit.

  • For first-time sightseeing: prioritise a direct route into central London over a prestigious address.
  • For a late arrival: make the airport-to-hotel route simple enough to complete with luggage.
  • For an early departure: consider the final night near the departure route instead of paying for a taxi across London.
  • For families: a larger room or kitchen farther out can work, but price the extra daily transport and time.

Local planning rule: search the hotel journey at the time you expect to make it, not at midday on a perfect weekday.

Build days around free anchors

Many major museums and galleries have free general admission, although exhibitions may charge and timed entry can still be useful. Parks, markets, neighbourhood walks and skyline viewpoints can fill the gaps without making the day feel like a list of compromises.

Westminster and the South Bank

Walk past major landmarks, cross the Thames and continue along the river instead of paying to move between nearby sights.

South Kensington museums

Choose one or two institutions rather than trying to “complete” all of them. Confirm current entry arrangements first.

Greenwich

Make the journey part of the day, explore the park and market, and compare the cost of the river with rail before deciding.

A realistic two-day budget structure

Day one: the classic central walk

Start around Westminster, cross towards the South Bank and follow the river east. Choose one paid attraction at most. Use a free gallery or market as the flexible part of the day, then eat away from the most obvious landmark frontage.

For breakfasts, picnic lunches and selected dinners, use our guide to saving money on food in the UK rather than paying tourist-area prices for every meal.

Day two: one neighbourhood cluster

Pick either South Kensington, Greenwich, Camden and Regent’s Park, or the City and East End. Staying in one cluster cuts transport, protects time and leaves room for something unplanned.

This is a researched two-day framework. It has not been field tested as a timed route. For a longer stay, our London and York itinerary shows how to add a second base without crowding every day.

Arriving at Heathrow instead of Gatwick? Compare the airport route before choosing your room. Our contactless and Oyster guide covers foreign cards and children.

Five budget mistakes that make London worse

  1. Booking the flight before pricing the airport transfer. “London” airports are not interchangeable.
  2. Changing payment method on transport. That can prevent journeys from counting towards one cap.
  3. Buying an attraction pass before choosing attractions. Price the actual plan first.
  4. Crossing London repeatedly. Group sights by area and leave space for walking.
  5. Choosing a distant hotel from room price alone. Add daily fare, journey time and late-night convenience.
