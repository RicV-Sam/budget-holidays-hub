Choose UK accommodation by location and total cost

Compare the room, the route and the facilities together, whether you are booking a city hotel or a countryside base.

Published and sources checked 15 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based

The short answer

Start with where you will spend your days and how you will reach the accommodation. Compare the total for the same dates and party, adding transfers, daily travel, parking and required extras. A lower nightly rate is only a saving if those extra costs and journeys still work for you.

Choose the base before choosing the room

Mark your main activities, arrival airport or station and onward departure. Then check the actual route from each shortlisted property. Distance on a map does not tell you about an interchange, steep approach or awkward station exit.

  • City sightseeing: compare a central room with a cheaper outer base, including the return journey each day and the last service home.
  • Early flight: compare a final airport night with travelling from your existing base. Confirm the terminal transfer and hotel access at the required hour.
  • Countryside: check whether you can reach meals and activities without a car, or whether parking and driving are part of the plan.

Use the airport comparison before committing to a base far from your arrival point.

How do you compare the full cost?

Use one currency, the same dates, the same party and the same room requirements. Compare final checkout totals, not a search result's nightly headline.

AccommodationAll nights and rooms, plus mandatory charges shown in the final quote.
Arrival and departureTransfers for the whole party, including the final connection to the entrance.
Daily travelThe journeys your actual itinerary requires; allow for child fares or passes only where eligible.

Meals and facilitiesBreakfast, cooking equipment, laundry or baggage storage you expect to use.
Booking flexibilityCancellation deadline, time zone, refund conditions and prepayment.

Illustration, not a price quote: saving £20 per night for four nights saves £80. If the cheaper location adds £100 in transport for the whole stay, it costs £20 more overall. Add travel time separately rather than treating it as free.

Carry the chosen total into the seven-day trip budget. Do not count transfers twice if you already included them there.

Check the journey to the actual entrance

Look at the walk from the stop, the crossing points and which station exit you need. Test an arrival route and an evening return, not only a midday journey. If a lift or boarding ramp matters, check the whole chain and live disruption. TfL distinguishes street-to-platform from street-to-train access; the first does not guarantee level boarding. TfL: wheelchair access and avoiding stairs.

Choose a location that fits your own walking distance, luggage and rest needs. A simpler journey can be worth more than a shorter advertised journey with several changes. The family and accessible transfer guide explains what to confirm.

Does the room actually fit the stay?

Check the booked room category, not just the hotel's general facilities. Confirm the number of real beds, sofa beds or cots, whether connecting rooms are guaranteed, and the occupancy rules for your children's ages. Request confirmation of anything essential before paying a non-refundable rate.

For self-catering, ask what cooking facilities, fridge space, utensils and laundry are actually provided. Compare grocery savings with the location and any extra fees. See food shopping for your accommodation facilities.

For access requirements, request the property's detailed accessibility guide and confirm the specific room: entrance steps, lift dimensions where relevant, bathroom layout, bed access and any equipment you need. VisitEngland promotes detailed descriptions, measurements and photos so visitors can judge suitability themselves. VisitEngland: detailed accessibility information. A general “accessible” filter is a starting point.

Parking and check-in can change the decision

Confirm whether parking is on site, reserved or first-come-first-served, and whether it belongs to another operator. Ask about height restrictions, overnight charges, entry/exit rules and vehicle registration. “Parking nearby” does not guarantee a space when you arrive.

Check the booking's arrival window, luggage-storage arrangements and late check-in procedure. Supplier conditions differ: Premier Inn's published terms include a 3pm standard check-in and advance notification for arrivals after 11pm. Premier Inn: booking terms. Ask your own property for its rules and use the first-day arrival checklist.

If a city stay comes before a rural trip, compare collecting your rental car later rather than paying to park it unused.

Is changing hotels worth it?

Compare the travel saved with the effort of packing, checking out, storing bags, transferring and checking in again. One well-placed base may work better for short stays; a second base can make sense when it removes repeated long return journeys. The London and York itinerary shows one existing two-base example.
