UK Trip Cost for 7 Days: A Budget You Can Adapt

Build a seven-day UK holiday budget with itemised GBP examples for solo visitors and couples, six nights of accommodation and clear exclusions.

Published and researched 5 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based · England, Wales and Scotland

The short answer

Our worked example comes to £748 per person for seven days and six nights when two adults share a £120 room. A solo traveller paying for that room alone would need £1,108. These are planning assumptions, not market averages or live quotes; international flights, entry permission and insurance are extra.

What this budget includes

The example is a London stay with a regional rail excursion or transfer allowance. It assumes seven calendar days, six paid nights, two adults sharing one room, economical meals and selected paid sights. All amounts are in pounds sterling. A seven-night holiday needs another night of accommodation, and possibly another day of spending.

Start with accommodation quotes for your real dates. A room price belongs to the room, not each guest: divide only when somebody is actually sharing the bill. Solo travellers should not budget using a couple’s per-person room cost.

For both adults, the base total is £1,496. Add a 15% planning contingency: £112.20 each, taking the example to £860.20 per person or £1,720.40 for two. A contingency is our suggested buffer, not an insurance substitute or a guarantee that a particular trip will fit.

The £9 local-transport line is an allowance, not a universal fare cap. Airport trips, different zones and premium services can cost more. Check your actual TfL journeys before relying on it.

Adjust the example to your trip

If the room quote is £180 rather than £120, six nights add £180 per person when sharing. If you travel alone in the £120 room, accommodation becomes £720 and the base total becomes £1,108. Staying only in London and removing the £60 regional rail allowance reduces the shared-room example to £688.

For London plus York, price each accommodation base separately and replace the rail allowance with both intercity tickets. Do not assume a regional hotel is cheaper on a festival or race weekend. Our seven-day route explains the overnight split and return journey.

Formula: your share of all room nights + daily food × days + local journeys + airport transfers + intercity tickets + attractions + contingency. Keep flights and permission costs on separate lines so a visitor from any country can use the same ground budget.

Costs to add before you book

  • International flights, baggage, seat selection and travel to your departure airport.
  • Any visa or ETA required for your circumstances; use the official checker rather than assuming everyone pays the same fee.
  • Travel insurance, mobile data and any foreign-card charges.
  • Accommodation deposits, breakfast supplements, luggage storage and late check-in costs where applicable.
  • Shopping, alcohol, theatre tickets and activities beyond the attractions allowance.

A rental car needs its own calculation: hire, fuel, parking, insurance terms and any applicable road charges. This public-transport example does not cover those costs.

Where to reduce cost without breaking the trip

First compare nearby dates and room types. Then check whether the cheaper airport still saves money after transfers. Keep long rail journeys to a minimum and compare Advance tickets only if you can commit to the specified train. A delayed inbound flight makes a tightly timed separate train booking less attractive.

Use supermarkets for breakfasts and picnic lunches when facilities permit. Choose free museum collections and walks before buying an attraction pass. A pass only saves money against attractions you would otherwise pay to enter.

Once the room and transport quotes are known, replace every allowance in the table. If the total is uncomfortable, shorten the route or reduce paid activities before cutting the buffer to zero.
