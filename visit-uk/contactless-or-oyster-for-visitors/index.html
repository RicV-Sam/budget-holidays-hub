Contactless or Oyster for Overseas Visitors to London?

Choose how to pay for London transport, with foreign-card checks, family considerations, fare caps and practical advice on tapping correctly.

Published and researched 5 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based · England, Wales and Scotland

The short answer

For an adult with an accepted contactless card and suitable bank fees, contactless is a convenient starting choice. Oyster can help if your card is unsuitable or a relevant discount is available. Check children separately, and use one consistent card or device per person throughout each journey.

No payment method is automatically cheapest for every itinerary. Calculate card purchase costs, any discounts and bank charges as well as fares. Do not order a Visitor Oyster solely because the word “visitor” appears in the name.

Will a foreign bank card work?

TfL says many overseas contactless cards can be used, but acceptance is not universal and overseas charges can apply. Check your issuer, including whether a mobile wallet linked to the card is supported. Keep a backup payment option accessible.

If a card fails, do not assume another reader will solve it. Ask staff for help and obtain a valid payment method or ticket before travelling. A phone or watch also needs enough battery for the journey.

How to tap without splitting your journey

  1. Choose one card or device for each traveller.
  2. On rail and the Tube, touch in and out as required, even when a gate is open.
  3. On buses and trams, only touch in.
  4. Use that same card or device consistently; a physical card and its phone-wallet version are treated separately.
  5. Keep other cards away from the reader to avoid card clash.

Two people cannot share one Oyster card for a journey. A couple may use separate accepted cards, or separate supported devices, but each traveller should keep their chosen method consistent. Do not pass a single card back through the gates for the next person.

What a fare cap does and does not cover

A cap limits eligible pay-as-you-go spending for the relevant period and travel area. It does not turn every journey into a free trip after you have spent a central London amount. Airport services, travel outside your zones and other transport products require their own checks.

Weekly capping runs Monday to Sunday, not an arbitrary seven days starting when you land. For a stay spanning two calendar weeks, compare your actual journeys instead of assuming the weekly cap applies once to the whole holiday.

Use TfL’s fare finder for a journey and its caps tools for your zones. Before paying for a paper Travelcard, compare it with pay as you go using the same route and dates.

Check children before buying adult fares

TfL has separate child and visitor-discount rules. Some children travel free on specified services, while older children may benefit from a Young Visitor discount added by staff to an Oyster or Visitor Oyster card. This is not a discount automatically applied to an adult bank card.

Check the age, service, accompanying-adult conditions and current process using TfL’s free and discounted travel guidance. Airport rail and National Rail services may have different conditions. Allow time to ask staff before your first paid journey rather than buying several adult cards in a hurry.

Three practical arrival decisions

Solo adult arriving at Heathrow: check the card with your bank, choose the rail route that fits your hotel and compare its fare. Our Heathrow guide explains the route trade-offs.

Two adults arriving at Gatwick: compare live train tickets with eligible pay-as-you-go fares. A special rail offer can change the answer. Each person needs their own payment method for tapping.

Family with an 11–15-year-old: check the child discount first, then compare the whole family cost. A convenient adult setup is not necessarily the best child arrangement. Keep transport in your trip budget separate from meals and sights so these choices remain visible.
