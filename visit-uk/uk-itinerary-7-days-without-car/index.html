7 Days in the UK Without a Car: London and York

A manageable first UK itinerary with three London nights, two York nights and a final London night, plus rail booking and arrival advice.

Published and researched 5 September 2026 · Research method

By Ric Vallaro · UK-based · England, Wales and Scotland

The short answer

For seven days and six nights with a London arrival and departure, use three nights in London, two in York and the final night back in London. This gives you two contrasting places and avoids a long intercity journey immediately before your flight. It is a suggested route, not a field-tested timetable.

Day one and day seven are travel days, not full sightseeing days. If your flight lands late, treat day one as arrival only. A departure from another city needs a different final-night plan. Do not book accommodation until the rail and flight timings work together.

Why London and York work as a first route

This is our editorial choice for a trip with two distinct bases: London for major sights and York for a smaller historic city. Both fit a rail-based holiday. It does not attempt to cover England, Wales and Scotland in one week.

Pick accommodation by the actual walk from the station. A historic centre can involve uneven surfaces, stairs or luggage friction even when distances look short. Check access directly with the accommodation if needed.

If Scotland is the main reason for visiting, build a separate route around Edinburgh rather than adding it to this week. Likewise, give Wales its own transport plan; rural places may need buses, tours or a car. Our UK planning hub helps you choose a realistic second region.

Book the rail legs before the sightseeing

Search London King’s Cross to York and the return separately in National Rail. Use the actual travel day; confirm whether the result is direct and whether engineering work introduces changes or replacement buses. We deliberately do not promise a single journey time for every date.

Advance tickets are tied to the specified train. Compare their savings with the flexibility you need. Book the day-four rail journey after three London nights rather than immediately following an international flight, reducing the risk that a delayed flight ruins a separate fixed train booking.

Keep day six flexible enough for a disruption. The final London night creates a buffer before departure, but it does not guarantee a connection. Check your airport route too. Reserve a seat when available and check the operator’s luggage and assistance information before travelling.

Keep each sightseeing day geographically compact

London day two: group Westminster and the South Bank. Walking between nearby sights can save fares and prevent repeated crossings of the city. Decide in advance which attraction, if any, is worth paying for; allow queues and breaks.

London day three: choose one museum area or neighbourhood rather than another circuit of distant landmarks. General collections and temporary exhibitions may have different charges or booking requirements.

York day five: the National Railway Museum is a possible indoor anchor. Visit York lists general entry as free; check current booking and opening details before going. Combine it with time in the city rather than fitting every attraction into the day. Historic walks can be less comfortable in rain or with mobility needs, so plan an indoor alternative.

Build a budget for this exact route

Price four London room nights and two York nights, including the separate final London booking. Add seven days of meals, both airport transfers, the return rail trip, local travel and chosen attractions. Divide room costs between the actual occupants; keep train tickets per person.

The seven-day cost example is a starting worksheet, not a quote for this itinerary. Replace its accommodation and rail allowances with the offers you can book. If York makes the total too high, a single London base with a modest day trip is a sensible alternative.

A kitchen or fridge can help with breakfasts, but check the room facilities rather than assuming every accommodation has them. Our supermarket guide gives food ideas for different facilities.

Booking order

  1. Check entry requirements and flight dates.
  2. Confirm airport-to-hotel routes and late check-in arrangements.
  3. Compare both rail legs and six nights of accommodation together.
  4. Read cancellation conditions before paying.
  5. Book only the attractions that matter to you.
  6. Recheck transport and opening information before departure.
